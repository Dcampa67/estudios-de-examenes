## 1. Unit 1: Childhood

**What the exam asks:** open-ended **writing prompts** about **childhood** — about **your own experiences** and/or **what you think will come in the future** in your life.

**Childhood** = the time of your life when you are a child (from being a baby until you are about 12–13).

### Possible prompts (practice answering them)
- What is your favorite memory from when you were little? Why?
- Who was the most important person in your childhood? Why?
- What is something you learned as a child that you still use today?
- How are you different now from when you were 6 years old?
- What do you think your life will be like in 10 years?
- What advice would you give to a younger kid?
- What is a challenge you faced as a child, and how did you overcome it?

### How to prepare (so you're never blank)
Before the exam, have **3 "ready" memories** in your head. For each one, know:
1. **What** happened (the event)
2. **Who** was there
3. **Where and when**
4. **How you felt** (use strong words: *desperate, disgusted, proud...*)
5. **Why it matters** to you / what you learned ← this is the "why" your teacher wants

And **1 idea about the future**: what you want to be, where you want to live, what you want to achieve — and **why**.

⚠️ **Twist:** the teacher cares about **how** you write (see section 3) as much as **what** you write. A great memory with no indent and 3 sentences loses points.

## 2. Vocabulary (12 words)

You need to: **know what each word means**, **use it in a sentence**, and **complete fill-in-the-blank sentences**.

---

### 1. Reflect *(verb)*
- **Definition:** to **think carefully and deeply** about something, especially about something that already happened.
- **En español:** **reflexionar**
- **Real-life examples:**
  - *"After the fight with my brother, I sat in my room to **reflect** on what I said."*
  - *"Mom, can I **reflect** on it tonight and tell you tomorrow if I want to join the team?"*
- **Word family:** reflection (noun) — *"I wrote a reflection about my summer."*
- ⚠️ **Twist:** *reflect* can also mean to **bounce back light** (a mirror reflects your image). In this unit, it means **to think**.

### 2. Notable *(adjective)*
- **Definition:** **important or interesting** enough to be **noticed** or remembered.
- **En español:** **notable, destacado, importante**
- **Real-life examples:**
  - *"The most **notable** thing about my trip was seeing the ocean for the first time."*
  - *"Bro, that goal was **notable** — everyone was talking about it after recess."*
- ⚠️ **Twist:** it comes from *note* → something **worth noticing**.

### 3. Contribute *(verb)*
- **Definition:** to **give** or **add** something (help, ideas, money, work) to help achieve something.
- **En español:** **contribuir, aportar**
- **Real-life examples:**
  - *"Everyone in my group has to **contribute** to the project — I'll make the poster."*
  - *"I **contributed** 50 pesos so we could buy a cake for our teacher."*
- **Word family:** contribution (noun)
- ⚠️ **Twist:** we say "contribute **to** something" (not "contribute in").

### 4. Recognize *(verb)*
- **Definition:** 1) to **know who someone is or what something is** because you've seen it before; 2) to **accept or appreciate** something.
- **En español:** **reconocer**
- **Real-life examples:**
  - *"I didn't **recognize** you with your new haircut!"*
  - *"My coach **recognized** my hard work and made me captain."*
- **Word family:** recognition (noun)

### 5. Memorize *(verb)*
- **Definition:** to **learn something so well** that you can remember it exactly.
- **En español:** **memorizar, aprenderse de memoria**
- **Real-life examples:**
  - *"I have to **memorize** the Ten Commandments for Friday."*
  - *"Can you test me? I think I **memorized** all the capitals."*
- ⚠️ **Twist:** *memorize* (learn by heart) ≠ *remember* (bring back to your mind).

### 6. Respected *(adjective)*
- **Definition:** **admired and valued** by others because of your good qualities or what you've done.
- **En español:** **respetado**
- **Real-life examples:**
  - *"My grandpa is very **respected** in our family — everyone listens to his advice."*
  - *"She's the most **respected** player on the team because she always plays fair."*
- **Word family:** respect (noun/verb), respectful (adjective = *respetuoso*)
- ⚠️ **Twist:** *respected* = others respect **you**. *respectful* = **you** show respect to others. Don't mix them!

### 7. Desperate *(adjective)*
- **Definition:** feeling that you are in a **very bad situation** and will **try anything** to change it; needing or wanting something **very, very much**.
- **En español:** **desesperado**
- **Real-life examples:**
  - *"I was **desperate** to find my phone — I looked everywhere, even in the fridge."*
  - *"After the game I was so thirsty, I was **desperate** for water."*
- ⚠️ **Twist (false friend!):** in Spanish, "desesperado" can also mean **impatient** ("me desespera esperar"). In English, **desperate does NOT mean impatient**. For that, say ***impatient***.

### 8. Disgusted *(adjective)*
- **Definition:** feeling that something is **very gross or unpleasant**, or strongly **disapproving** of something.
- **En español:** **asqueado, con asco** (también **indignado**)
- **Real-life examples:**
  - *"I was **disgusted** when I found a hair in my soup."*
  - *"The whole class was **disgusted** when they saw someone throw trash in the garden."*
- ⚠️ **Twist (false friend!):** Spanish "disgustado" means **upset/annoyed**. English ***disgusted*** means **grossed out** ("¡qué asco!"). Very different!

### 9. Imperative *(adjective)*
- **Definition:** **extremely important** and **necessary**; something that **must** be done.
- **En español:** **imprescindible, indispensable, urgente**
- **Real-life examples:**
  - *"It's **imperative** that I study tonight — my Religion exam is tomorrow."*
  - *"Coach said it's **imperative** to drink water before the game."*
- ⚠️ **Twist:** it's often used as ***"It is imperative that..."*** or ***"It is imperative to..."***. It's stronger than just "important".

### 10. Candor *(noun)*
- **Definition:** the quality of being **honest, open, and direct**, even when the truth is uncomfortable.
- **En español:** **franqueza, sinceridad, honestidad**
- **Real-life examples:**
  - *"I appreciate your **candor**, Mom — it's true that I didn't study enough."*
  - *"My friend answered with **candor**: 'Honestly, your drawing needs more color.'"*
- **Word family:** candid (adjective) = honest, direct
- ⚠️ **Twist (false friend!):** in Spanish, "candor" means **innocence / naivety**. In English, **candor = honesty**. Don't translate it as "inocencia"!

### 11. Diligent *(adjective)*
- **Definition:** showing **care and effort** in your work; **hardworking** and careful.
- **En español:** **diligente, trabajador, dedicado, aplicado**
- **Real-life examples:**
  - *"I've been very **diligent** this week — I finished all my study guides."*
  - *"My sister is so **diligent** that she does her homework the same day she gets it."*
- **Word family:** diligence (noun)

### 12. Persistence *(noun)*
- **Definition:** **continuing to try** to do something even when it's **difficult** or when you fail; **not giving up**.
- **En español:** **persistencia, perseverancia, constancia**
- **Real-life examples:**
  - *"It took me 20 tries to do a kickflip, but with **persistence** I finally did it."*
  - *"My dad says **persistence** is more important than talent."*
- **Word family:** persist (verb), persistent (adjective)
- ⚠️ **Twist:** *persistence* is a **noun** (a thing). If the sentence needs an adjective, use ***persistent***: *"She is **persistent**."*

### 🧠 How to solve fill-in-the-blank
1. **Read the whole sentence** first.
2. **Look at the word before the blank** to know the **type of word** you need:
   - After **"to"** or **"I / you / we / they"** → probably a **verb** (*reflect, contribute, recognize, memorize*).
   - After **"is / was / very / so / felt"** → probably an **adjective** (*notable, respected, desperate, disgusted, imperative, diligent*).
   - After **"his / her / your / with / the"** → probably a **noun** (*candor, persistence*).
3. **Look for clue words** in the sentence (*honest* → candor; *gross* → disgusted; *didn't give up* → persistence; *must* → imperative).
4. **Read it again** with your word to see if it sounds right.

**Example:** *"Thanks to her ________, she finally passed the test after failing twice."*
→ After "her" → noun. Clue: "after failing twice" = didn't give up → **persistence** ✅

⚠️ **Twist — the 3 false friends:** **candor** (≠ inocencia), **disgusted** (≠ disgustado), **desperate** (≠ impaciente).

## 3. Writing Structure

These are the **5 habits** your teacher wants in **every** piece of writing. Memorize the list — you might have to **recall/list** them.

| # | Rule | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Printing at all times** | Write in **print (letra de molde)**, **not cursive**, so it's easy to read |
| 2 | **Indenting** | Leave a **space at the beginning of every new paragraph** (sangría) |
| 3 | **Complete sentences: 5 or more per paragraph** | Each sentence has a **subject + verb** and a complete idea; **at least 5** per paragraph |
| 4 | **Going above and beyond** | Be **detailed and descriptive**: use colors, feelings, sounds, specific names, strong words |
| 5 | **Providing the "why"** | **Explain your thinking**: give reasons and logic (use *because, that's why, this shows that...*) |

🧠 Trick to remember them: **P-I-C-A-W** → **P**rint · **I**ndent · **C**omplete sentences (5+) · **A**bove and beyond · **W**hy
→ "**PICA** the **W**hy" 🌶️

### Complete sentence vs. fragment
- ❌ Fragment: *"Because I was scared."* (not a complete idea)
- ✅ Complete: *"I didn't want to go in the pool because I was scared."*

### Basic vs. above and beyond
- ❌ Basic: *"I went to the beach. It was fun."*
- ✅ Above and beyond: *"When I was seven, my family took me to the beach in Acapulco, where I saw the ocean for the first time. The sand was so hot that I had to run on my tiptoes, and the waves sounded like thunder."*

### Without the "why" vs. with the "why"
- ❌ *"My grandma is my favorite person."*
- ✅ *"My grandma is my favorite person **because** she always has time to listen to me, even when she is tired."*

### ✍️ Model paragraph (Childhood)

> ⠀⠀⠀⠀One of the most notable memories from my childhood is the day I learned to ride a bike without training wheels. I was six years old, and my dad took me to the park near our house on a sunny Saturday morning. At first I fell so many times that my knees were scratched and I was desperate to give up. My dad told me that persistence is what makes people succeed, so I kept trying again and again. After almost an hour, I finally rode all the way to the big tree by myself while my dad cheered and clapped. When I reflect on that day, I realize it taught me that I can do difficult things if I don't quit. That is why, even now, when something is hard at school, I remember that park and keep going.

**Why this paragraph works:**
- ✅ **Indent** at the beginning
- ✅ **7 complete sentences** (more than 5)
- ✅ **Details**: age, place, time, scratched knees, the big tree
- ✅ **The "why"**: what it taught me and why it still matters
- ✅ **Uses vocabulary**: notable, desperate, persistence, reflect
- ✅ It's a **true story** with **beginning, middle, and end** → a **nonfiction narrative**!

## 4. Nonfiction Narrative

**Definition:** a type of writing in which an author **tells a true story**.

### The 3 key aspects (how to identify it)
| # | Aspect | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **It's a true story** | It presents **accurate information** about **real people** and **real events** |
| 2 | **Two goals: to inform and to entertain** | It teaches you something real **and** it's enjoyable to read, like a story |
| 3 | **A clear beginning, middle, and end** | It's told like a story, in order |

🧠 Trick: **T-I-B** → **T**rue · **I**nform + entertain · **B**eginning–middle–end.

### Examples
| Nonfiction narrative ✅ | Not a nonfiction narrative ❌ |
|---|---|
| An autobiography or memoir (the story of your own life) | A fairy tale (not true) |
| A biography (the true story of someone's life) | A science textbook (informs, but it's not a story) |
| A true story in a newspaper about a rescue | A fantasy novel like Harry Potter (invented) |
| Your paragraph about learning to ride a bike | A recipe or instructions (no story) |

⚠️ **Twists:**
- **Nonfiction** = **true**. **Fiction** = **invented**. (Tricky because "non" sounds negative, but nonfiction is the **real** one!)
- A text can be **true** and still **not** be a narrative — an encyclopedia article is true, but it doesn't **tell a story** with beginning, middle, and end.
- It must have **both** goals: **inform AND entertain**. Only informing = regular informational text.

## 6. Additional Topics

For each one, know **what it means** and **why it's important at school and at home**.

| Topic | What it means | At school | At home | Why it's important |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Accountability** | **Accepting the consequences** of your actions and **admitting your mistakes** — no excuses, no blaming others | "I didn't do my homework. It was my fault, and I'll do it tonight." | "I broke the glass. I'm sorry — I'll help clean it up." | People **trust** you, and you **learn** from mistakes |
| **Personal Responsibility** | **Doing what you're supposed to do** on your own, **without being told** | Doing homework, studying for exams, paying attention | Making your bed, helping with chores, taking care of your things | It shows you're **mature** and **reliable** |
| **Class Rules** | The **rules that keep the classroom safe, respectful, and ready to learn** | Raise your hand, be on time, respect others, listen when others speak | (House rules work the same way) | Everyone can **learn** and feel **safe and respected** |
| **Organization** | **Keeping your things, time, and work in order** | A tidy backpack, labeled notebooks, using an agenda for due dates | A clean room, a schedule for homework and free time | You **don't lose** things, you **save time**, and you have **less stress** |
| **Preparation** | **Getting ready ahead of time** | Bringing your materials, studying before the exam (not the night before!), doing homework on time | Packing your backpack the night before | You **do better** and feel **confident** |

⚠️ **Twist — Accountability vs. Personal Responsibility:**
- **Responsibility** is **before**: doing your job. *"I'll study for the exam."*
- **Accountability** is **after**: owning the result. *"I didn't study enough, and that's why I got a low grade. Next time I'll start earlier."*
