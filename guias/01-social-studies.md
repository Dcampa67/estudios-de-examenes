## 1. What is Social Studies?

**Definition:** Social Studies is the study of **people and society**: how people live, how they get along with each other, how they organize themselves, and how they interact with the places where they live — in the past and in the present.

It is not just one subject. It is a group of subjects together:

| Subject | What it studies | Example question |
|---|---|---|
| **History** | What happened in the past | Why did Mexico become independent? |
| **Geography** | Places and the Earth | Where is the Sonoran Desert? |
| **Civics / Government** | Rules, laws, and how countries are run | Who makes the laws in Mexico? |
| **Economics** | Money, work, buying and selling | Why do prices go up? |
| **Culture (Anthropology / Sociology)** | Traditions, groups, how people behave | Why do we celebrate Día de Muertos? |

**Why it matters:** it helps us understand the world, make good decisions as citizens, and respect other people and cultures.

⚠️ **Twist:** Geography is **part of** Social Studies. If a question asks "Is geography a part of Social Studies?" → **Yes.**

## 2. What is Geography?

**Definition:** Geography is the study of the **Earth**: its places, its physical features (mountains, rivers, climates), and the **relationship between people and their environment**.

- The word comes from Greek: **geo** = Earth + **graphy** = to write/describe → "describing the Earth."

There are two big branches:

| Physical geography | Human geography |
|---|---|
| Natural things | Things made or done by people |
| Mountains, rivers, climate, biomes, volcanoes | Cities, countries, languages, roads, population |
| Example: the Popocatépetl volcano | Example: Mexico City's population |

**Absolute vs. relative location** (useful for the coordinates section):
- **Absolute location** = the exact spot, using coordinates. *Mexico City is at about 19°N, 99°W.*
- **Relative location** = where something is **compared to** something else. *Guatemala is south of Mexico.*

⭐ **Bonus — the 5 Themes of Geography** (may not be on the exam, but they help): **Location, Place, Human-Environment Interaction, Movement, Region.**

⚠️ **Twist:** "Geography is only about maps" → **False.** It is also about people and how they use and change the land.

## 3. Latitude, Longitude, the Equator, and the Prime Meridian

Imagine the Earth covered in a grid of invisible lines. That grid lets us find any place.

### Latitude (parallels)
- Lines that go **horizontally** (left to right, like the steps of a **ladder**).
- They measure how far **north or south** a place is from the Equator.
- They go from **0° (Equator) to 90°N** (North Pole) and **90°S** (South Pole).
- They are also called **parallels** because they never touch each other.
- 🧠 Memory trick: **"Lat is flat"** → latitude lines are flat (horizontal). "LADDER-tude."

### Longitude (meridians)
- Lines that go **vertically** (top to bottom, from pole to pole).
- They measure how far **east or west** a place is from the Prime Meridian.
- They go from **0° (Prime Meridian) to 180°E** and **180°W**.
- They are also called **meridians**. They all **meet at the poles**.
- 🧠 Memory trick: **"Longitude lines are LONG"** — they stretch from the North Pole to the South Pole.

### The Equator
- The **0° line of latitude**.
- It divides the Earth into the **Northern Hemisphere** and the **Southern Hemisphere**.
- It is the **longest** line of latitude. Places near it are usually **hot**.

### The Prime Meridian
- The **0° line of longitude**.
- It passes through **Greenwich, London (England)**.
- It divides the Earth into the **Eastern Hemisphere** and the **Western Hemisphere**.
- On the other side of the planet, at **180°**, is the **International Date Line**.

| | Latitude | Longitude |
|---|---|---|
| Direction of the lines | Horizontal (east–west) | Vertical (north–south) |
| What it measures | North or South | East or West |
| 0° line | Equator | Prime Meridian |
| Maximum | 90° | 180° |
| Other name | Parallels | Meridians |
| Do the lines touch? | Never | Yes, at the poles |

⚠️ **Twists:**
- Latitude lines **run** east–west, but they **measure** north–south. (This is the #1 trap!)
- Longitude lines **run** north–south, but they **measure** east–west.
- The Equator is a line of **latitude**, **not** longitude.
- There is no "90°E longitude as the maximum" — longitude goes up to **180°**. Latitude only goes up to **90°**.
- **Mexico** is in the **Northern** and **Western** hemispheres (north of the Equator, west of the Prime Meridian).

## 4. Coordinates

**Coordinates** are the "address" of a place on Earth using latitude and longitude.

**The rule:** always write **latitude first, then longitude**.
- Format: **(latitude N/S, longitude E/W)**
- 🧠 Trick: **alphabetical order** → **La**titude comes before **Lo**ngitude. Or: "you climb the **ladder** (latitude) before you walk **along** (longitude)."

**Examples:**
| Place | Coordinates (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Mexico City | 19°N, 99°W |
| Cancún | 21°N, 87°W |
| London | 51°N, 0° |
| Sydney, Australia | 34°S, 151°E |

**How to find a place with coordinates (step by step):**
1. Look at latitude: go **up** (N) or **down** (S) from the Equator to that number.
2. Look at longitude: go **right** (E) or **left** (W) from the Prime Meridian to that number.
3. Where the two lines cross — that's your place.

⚠️ **Twists:**
- **Always** include the letter (N, S, E, W). "19°, 99°" is incomplete — it could be 4 different places!
- Swapping the order is wrong: (99°W, 19°N) is written backwards.
- Degrees can be divided into **minutes (')**: 1° = 60'. Example: 19°26'N.
- A place on the Equator has latitude **0°** (no N or S needed).

## 5. Map Projections

**The problem:** the Earth is a **sphere** (round), but maps are **flat**. It's like peeling an orange and trying to flatten the peel — it rips or stretches.

**Map projection** = a way of showing the round Earth on a flat surface.

**Key ideas:**
- **Every flat map has distortion** — something is not exactly right: the **shape**, the **size**, the **distance**, or the **direction** of places.
- Each projection chooses what to keep correct and what to sacrifice. **No projection is perfect.**
- Only a **globe** shows the Earth without distortion (because it's round too).

**Example:** on many world maps, **Greenland looks as big as Africa**, but Africa is really about **14 times bigger**. That's distortion of **size** — places near the poles look bigger than they are.

⚠️ **Twists:**
- "Which is the most accurate representation of the Earth?" → a **globe**, not a map.
- Distortion doesn't mean the map is "wrong" or useless — each projection is good for something different (navigation, comparing sizes, etc.).
- Distortion is **biggest near the poles** and smallest near the Equator on most world maps.

## 6. Types of Maps

| Type | What it shows | Examples | Trick to remember |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reference map** | **General** information: borders, cities, rivers, mountains, roads | A political map of Mexico with states and capitals; a world map in an atlas | "**Reference** = look things up, general info" |
| **Thematic map** | **One specific topic (theme)** | Population map, climate map, rainfall map, map of COVID cases, map of languages | "**Theme** = ONE topic" |
| **Navigational map** | Helps you **travel / find a route** | Nautical charts for ships, airplane charts, road maps, Google Maps / Waze | "**Navigate** = get from A to B" |
| **Topographic map** | The **shape and elevation** of the land (how high or low it is) using **contour lines** | Maps for hikers, maps of mountains and volcanoes | "**Topo** = the ups and downs" |

**Contour lines (for topographic maps):**
- Each line connects points at the **same height**.
- Lines **close together** = **steep** (like a cliff).
- Lines **far apart** = **flat or gentle** slope.
- Circles inside circles = a **hill or mountain**.

⚠️ **Twists:**
- A **political map** (borders + capitals) is a type of **reference** map, not thematic.
- A **physical map** (mountains, rivers, colors for height) is also a **reference** map. A **topographic** map is more specific — it uses contour lines.
- A **weather/climate map** = **thematic** (one theme).
- A **road map** could be both reference and navigational — if the question says "used to plan a trip," answer **navigational**.

## 7. Biomes

**Biome** = a large area of the Earth with a similar **climate**, **plants**, and **animals**. Climate (temperature + rain) decides which biome it is.

### 🌴 Tropical Rainforest
- **Climate:** hot and **very wet all year** (lots of rain, often more than 2,000 mm per year). No real winter.
- **Location:** near the **Equator** — Amazon (South America), Congo (Africa), Southeast Asia. **In Mexico:** Selva Lacandona (Chiapas).
- **Plants:** tall trees, vines, orchids. It has **layers**: emergent layer (tallest trees) → canopy (the "roof") → understory → forest floor (dark, little sunlight).
- **Animals:** jaguars, monkeys, toucans, frogs, snakes, millions of insects.
- **Key fact:** it has the **most biodiversity** (most kinds of living things) of any biome. It produces lots of oxygen.
- **Threat:** deforestation.

### 🏜️ Desert
- **Climate:** **very little rain** — less than **250 mm per year**. That's the definition!
- **Location:** Sahara (Africa), **Sonoran and Chihuahuan deserts (Mexico)**, Atacama (Chile).
- **Plants:** cactus, agave, shrubs. Adaptations: thick stems that **store water**, spines instead of leaves (to lose less water), long or wide roots.
- **Animals:** snakes, lizards, scorpions, coyotes, camels. Many are **nocturnal** (active at night to avoid heat).
- **Temperature:** very hot days and **cold nights** (no clouds to keep the heat in).
- ⚠️ **Twist:** a desert is defined by **low rain**, **not** by heat. **Antarctica is the largest desert in the world** — and it's cold!

### 🌾 Grasslands
- **Climate:** **medium rain** — more than a desert, **not enough for a forest**. Dry season + wet season.
- **Two kinds:**
  - **Savanna (tropical grassland):** warm all year, grass with **scattered trees** — Africa (lions, zebras, giraffes, elephants).
  - **Temperate grassland:** hot summers, cold winters, almost no trees — called **prairies** (USA), **pampas** (Argentina), **steppes** (Russia/Asia). Animals: bison, prairie dogs.
- **Plants:** mostly grasses. **Fires** and **grazing animals** keep trees from growing.
- **Human use:** very fertile soil → great for farming (wheat, corn) and cattle.

### ❄️ Tundra
- **Climate:** the **coldest** biome. Very cold, long winters, **short cool summers**, little rain.
- **Location:** near the **Arctic** (Alaska, Canada, Russia, Greenland). **Alpine tundra** = on top of very high mountains (even in Mexico, near the peaks of volcanoes like the Pico de Orizaba).
- **Key word: Permafrost** = ground that stays **frozen all year** under the surface. Because of it, **trees can't grow** (roots can't go deep).
- **Plants:** mosses, lichens, small shrubs, grasses — all **short**.
- **Animals:** polar bears, arctic foxes, caribou/reindeer, snowy owls. Many **migrate** or have thick fur/fat.

### 🌊 Mangroves
- **What:** forests of trees that grow in **salty (salt) water** on tropical and subtropical **coasts**, where rivers meet the sea.
- **Location:** tropical coasts all over the world. **Mexico has one of the largest areas of mangroves in the world** (Yucatán Peninsula, Campeche, Nayarit, Chiapas).
- **Plants:** mangrove trees with **prop roots** (stilt roots) that stick up out of the water/mud, and roots that help them breathe and filter salt.
- **Animals:** fish, crabs, shrimp, crocodiles, birds (flamingos, pelicans).
- **Why they're important:**
  1. **Protect the coast** from hurricanes, waves, and erosion (like a natural wall).
  2. Are a **nursery** for baby fish and sea animals.
  3. Store a lot of carbon (help against climate change).
- ⚠️ **Twist:** most trees die in salt water — mangroves are special because they **can live in salt water**.

### Quick comparison
| Biome | Temperature | Rain | Trees? | Key word |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tropical rainforest | Hot | Very high | Yes, tall, in layers | Biodiversity |
| Desert | Hot days / cold nights (or cold) | Very low (<250 mm) | Almost none | Adaptation to save water |
| Grassland | Warm or hot/cold seasons | Medium | Few (savanna) or none | Grass, grazing, fires |
| Tundra | Very cold | Low | **No** | Permafrost |
| Mangrove | Warm | High, near the sea | Yes, in salt water | Prop roots, coast protection |

### 🧠 Memory tricks for the hard ones
- **"Mexi-CALI"** → Baja **CALI**fornia's capital is Mexi**cali** (it even has "Cali" in it!).
- **"La Paz is at the tip"** → Baja California **Sur** (south) = La Paz, at the bottom of the peninsula.
- **"Her-MOSILLO in SOnora"** → both have a lot of **O**'s.
- **"Culiacán — Sinaloa"** → Sinaloa's big beach city is Mazatlán, but the capital is **Culiacán** (both end in "-án", don't mix them).
- **"Te-PIC in Na-YA-RIT"** → Nayarit is small, Tepic is short.
- **"SALT-illo in COA-huila"** → imagine salt on a coa (a farming tool).
- **"Monterrey = Nuevo León"** → the "rey" (king) and the "león" (lion), the king of the jungle.
- **"Victoria in Tamaulipas"** → Tamaulipas **wins** (victory) the Gulf coast.
- **"Guadalajara, Jalisco"** → mariachi, tequila... you already know this one.
- **"MOR-elia in Michoacán, MOR-elos → Cuernavaca"** ⚠️ **Don't mix them up!** **Morelia** is the capital of Michoacán, **not** of Morelos. Morelos's capital is **Cuernavaca**.
- **"TOLUCA — the State of Mexico is next to CDMX"** — remember Toluca to avoid the trap.
- **"PACHUCA — Hidalgo"** → Pachuca's famous for pastes and soccer (Tuzos).
- **"CHIL-pancingo in Guerrero"** → a **guerrero** (warrior) who is **chill**.
- **"XALAPA — Veracruz"** → Xalapa gave its name to the **jalapeño** chile!
- **"VILLA-HERMOSA — Tabasco"** → a "beautiful town" with tabasco sauce.
- **"TUXTLA — Chiapas"** → both have a "**T**" and a "**CH**" sound... Tux-tla, Chia-pas.
- **"MÉRIDA — Yucatán"** → the white city.
- **"CHETUMAL — Quintana Roo"** → Cancún is for tourists, **Chetumal** is for the government.

### 📅 How to study them (suggested)
1. **Day 1:** learn the 12 easy ones (same name) — 10 minutes, you'll get them fast.
2. **Day 1:** learn the tricky ones **region by region** (one region, cover the capital column, say them out loud, then next region).
3. **Day 2:** cover the capital column of the whole list and test yourself. Then cover the **state** column and do it backwards (capital → state).
4. Write the ones you miss on a separate paper and repeat only those.

⚠️ **Top 5 traps (read these the night before!):**
1. Quintana Roo → **Chetumal**, not Cancún.
2. Baja California → **Mexicali**, not Tijuana.
3. Estado de México → **Toluca**, not Mexico City.
4. Veracruz → **Xalapa**, not Veracruz (port).
5. Morelia is **Michoacán**; Morelos is **Cuernavaca**.
