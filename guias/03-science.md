## 1. Introduction to Science

### What is science?
**Science** is a way of learning about the natural world by **observing, asking questions, testing ideas with experiments, and using evidence**.
- Science is not just a list of facts — it's a **method** (a way of finding out).
- Scientific ideas can **change** when new evidence appears.

### Tangible vs. intangible knowledge
| Tangible | Intangible |
|---|---|
| Can be **observed, measured, or tested** with our senses or tools | **Cannot** be measured or tested |
| The height of a plant, the temperature of water, the weight of a rock | Love, beauty, friendship, whether a song is "good", faith |
| **Science studies this** | Science **can't** test this |

⚠️ **Twist:** science not being able to test something **doesn't mean it isn't real** — love is real, but you can't measure it with a ruler.

### Truth / fact vs. opinion
- **Fact:** a statement that can be **proven true or false** with evidence. *"Water boils at 100 °C at sea level."*
- **Opinion:** what someone **thinks or feels**; it can't be proven. *"Science is the most fun subject."*
- 🧠 Trick: watch for opinion words — **best, worst, beautiful, boring, should, I think, I feel**.

⚠️ **Twist:** a fact can be **false**! "The Moon is made of cheese" is a *factual statement* (it can be tested) — it's just wrong. An opinion can't be tested at all.

### Observation vs. inference
- **Observation:** what you **directly notice with your senses** (see, hear, smell, touch, taste). No guessing.
- **Inference:** a **conclusion or explanation** you make **based on** observations + what you already know.

| Observation | Inference |
|---|---|
| The ground is wet. | It rained last night. |
| My friend is smiling and holding a trophy. | My friend won the game. |
| The plant's leaves are yellow and droopy. | The plant needs water. |
| There's smoke coming from the kitchen. | Something is burning. |

🧠 Trick: if you could be **wrong** about it (maybe someone watered the grass!), it's probably an **inference**.

⚠️ **Twist:** "The dog is happy" → **inference** (you see it wagging its tail — that's the observation).

### Variables
A **variable** is anything that can **change** in an experiment.

| Variable | What it is | Question to ask | 🧠 Trick |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Independent variable** | The one thing **I change on purpose** | "What am I changing?" | **I** change the **I**ndependent |
| **Dependent variable** | What I **measure** to see the result | "What am I measuring?" | It **depends** on the independent |
| **Controlled variables** | Everything I keep **the same** | "What stays the same?" | **Control** = keep it the same |

**The link:** the **dependent** variable **depends on** the **independent** variable.
Sentence to check: *"The [dependent] depends on the [independent]."* → "The **plant's height** depends on **the amount of sunlight**." ✅

### Evidence and fair tests
- **Evidence:** the **data / results** you collect (measurements, observations) that support or reject an idea.
- **Fair test:** an experiment where you **change only ONE variable** (the independent) and keep **everything else the same** (controlled).
- **Why?** If you change two things at once, you won't know which one caused the result.
- A good test also has a **control group** (the "normal" one you compare against) and is **repeated** several times to be reliable.

⚠️ **Twist:** Unfair test example: You give Plant A water + fertilizer, and Plant B nothing. Plant A grows more. Was it the water or the fertilizer? **You can't know** → not a fair test.

### How to make a prediction with a mechanism (hypothesis)
A **hypothesis** is a **testable prediction** that also explains **why** (the **mechanism**).

**Formula:** **If** [I change the independent variable], **then** [this will happen to the dependent variable], **because** [the reason / mechanism].

- ❌ Weak: *"The plant will grow."* (no variable, no reason)
- ❌ Still weak: *"If plants get more light, they will grow taller."* (no mechanism)
- ✅ Strong: *"**If** a plant gets more sunlight, **then** it will grow taller, **because** plants use light energy to make their food through photosynthesis."*

⚠️ **Twist:** the **"because"** part is the **mechanism** — that's what your teacher means by "a prediction with a mechanism". Don't forget it!

## 2. 🔬 Worked Examples: Experiments (practice this type of question)

### Example 1 — Plants and sunlight
> Sofía wants to know if the amount of sunlight affects how tall a bean plant grows. She uses 3 identical bean plants in the same kind of pot and soil. She puts one in full sun, one in half shade, and one in a dark closet. She gives each one 50 ml of water every day. After 2 weeks, she measures their height in centimeters.

| Question | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Independent variable | **Amount of sunlight** | It's the one thing she changes on purpose |
| Dependent variable | **Height of the plant (cm)** | It's what she measures |
| Controlled variables | Type of plant, pot, soil, amount of water (50 ml), time (2 weeks) | Everything kept the same |
| Hypothesis | **If** a bean plant gets more sunlight, **then** it will grow taller, **because** plants need light to make food by photosynthesis. | If–then–because |
| Is it a fair test? | **Yes** | Only one variable changes |
| Observation example | "The plant in the closet is 3 cm and pale yellow." | Seen and measured |
| Inference example | "The plant in the closet couldn't make enough food." | An explanation of the observation |

### Example 2 — Sugar dissolving
> Mateo drops one spoon of sugar into cups of water at 10 °C, 30 °C, and 60 °C. Each cup has 200 ml of water, and he stirs 10 times. He times how many seconds it takes for the sugar to disappear.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Independent variable | **Temperature of the water** |
| Dependent variable | **Time for the sugar to dissolve (seconds)** |
| Controlled variables | Amount of sugar (1 spoon), amount of water (200 ml), number of stirs (10), type of sugar, type of cup |
| Hypothesis | **If** the water is hotter, **then** the sugar will dissolve faster, **because** the water particles move faster and break the sugar apart more quickly. |

### Example 3 — Spot the mistake (unfair test)
> Ana wants to know which paper towel brand absorbs the most water. She dips Brand A in **50 ml** of water for **10 seconds** and Brand B in **100 ml** of water for **30 seconds**.

- **What's wrong?** She changed **three** things: the brand, the amount of water, and the time. ❌ Not a fair test.
- **How to fix it:** same amount of water and same time for both; **only the brand changes**.
- Independent: **brand of paper towel** · Dependent: **amount of water absorbed (ml)** · Controlled: amount of water, time, size of the paper towel.

### ✏️ Your turn
> Luis wants to know if the height from which he drops a ball affects how high it bounces. He drops the same tennis ball from 50 cm, 100 cm, and 150 cm onto the same floor and measures how high it bounces.

Find: independent variable, dependent variable, 2 controlled variables, and write a hypothesis with a mechanism.

<details>
<summary>Answers (try first!)</summary>

- **Independent:** the drop height
- **Dependent:** the bounce height (cm)
- **Controlled:** the same ball, the same floor (also: same person measuring, same ruler)
- **Hypothesis:** *If the ball is dropped from higher up, then it will bounce higher, because it falls faster and has more energy when it hits the floor.*
</details>

## 3. What is Life? — MRS GREN

To be **alive**, something must do **all 7** life processes:

| Letter | Process | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M** | **Movement** | Moving all or part of the body | A cat runs; a sunflower turns toward the sun |
| **R** | **Respiration** | Getting **energy** from food (inside the cells) | Your cells use food + oxygen to make energy |
| **S** | **Sensitivity** | Sensing and **responding** to the surroundings | You pull your hand away from something hot; roots grow toward water |
| **G** | **Growth** | Getting bigger / developing over time | A baby becomes an adult; a seed becomes a tree |
| **R** | **Reproduction** | Making **new living things** (offspring) | A dog has puppies; a plant makes seeds |
| **E** | **Excretion** | Getting rid of **waste** made by the body | Breathing out carbon dioxide; sweating; urine |
| **N** | **Nutrition** | Taking in or **making food** | Animals eat; plants make food by photosynthesis |

### Testing things with MRS GREN
| Thing | What it does | What it can't do | Alive? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Car** 🚗 | Moves, "takes in" gasoline, releases exhaust (smoke) | Doesn't grow, doesn't reproduce, doesn't sense by itself | ❌ **Not alive** |
| **Crystal** 💎 | Grows (gets bigger) | No movement, no respiration, no reproduction, no nutrition | ❌ **Not alive** |
| **Virus** 🦠 | Reproduces — **but only inside a living cell** | Can't do it alone; no respiration, no nutrition, no growth | ❌ Usually classified as **not alive** |
| **Fire** 🔥 | Grows, "eats" fuel, needs oxygen, makes waste (smoke), spreads | No sensitivity, not made of cells | ❌ **Not alive** |
| **Tree** 🌳 | All 7 (it moves slowly toward light) | — | ✅ **Alive** |

⚠️ **Twists:**
- Something must do **ALL 7**. Doing some of them is **not enough** (fire and cars do several!).
- **Plants DO move** — they just move slowly (leaves turn to the light, roots grow down).
- **Respiration ≠ breathing.** Respiration is getting **energy from food** inside cells. Plants respire too, and they don't have lungs.
- **Excretion ≠ only poop.** Breathing out CO₂ and sweating are excretion too.
- The **virus** is the favorite trick question: it reproduces, but **only by using another living cell**, so it's considered **not alive** (or "on the border of life").

## 4. Plants 1 — The 4 Characteristics of Plants

All plants share these **4 characteristics**:

| Characteristic | Meaning | 🧠 Trick |
|---|---|---|
| **Multicellular** | Made of **many cells** | multi = many |
| **Eukaryote** | Their cells have a **nucleus** (a "control center") | **Eu**karyote = cells that are "**u**p to date", with a nucleus |
| **Autotroph** | They **make their own food** (photosynthesis) | **auto** = self, **troph** = food → "self-feeder" |
| **Cell walls** | Each cell has a **stiff wall** made of **cellulose** around it, which gives support | Like a brick wall around each cell |

**Opposite words (useful for the twists):**
- Multicellular ↔ **Unicellular** (one cell, like bacteria)
- Eukaryote ↔ **Prokaryote** (no nucleus, like bacteria)
- Autotroph ↔ **Heterotroph** (must **eat** other living things for food, like animals and fungi)

⚠️ **Twists:**
- **Animals** are multicellular and eukaryotes too — but they are **heterotrophs** and have **no cell walls**. That's the difference!
- **Fungi** have cell walls too — but made of **chitin**, not cellulose, and they are **heterotrophs**.
- Plants also have **chlorophyll** (inside **chloroplasts**) — that's how they make food.

## 5. Plants 2 — Classifying Plants: What is NOT a plant?

To check if something is a plant, use the **4 characteristics**. If it fails **one**, it's **not a plant**.

| Looks like a plant... | What it really is | Why it's NOT a plant |
|---|---|---|
| **Mushroom** 🍄 | A **fungus** | It's a **heterotroph** (it can't make its own food; it absorbs food from dead things), has **no chlorophyll**, and its cell walls are made of **chitin**, not cellulose |
| **Seaweed** 🌊 | An **alga** (algae = protists) | It makes its own food, but it has **no real roots, stems, or leaves** and **no vascular tissue**; many algae are simple. It's in a different kingdom |
| **Lichen** 🪨 | A **partnership** of a **fungus + an alga** (or cyanobacteria) living together | It's **not one organism** — it's two different living things working together |
| **Coral** 🪸 | An **animal**! (tiny animals called **polyps**) | It's a **heterotroph** (eats plankton) and has **no cell walls** |

⚠️ **Twists:**
- **Coral is an animal**, even though it doesn't move and looks like a rock or a plant. (The colors come from tiny algae that live inside it.)
- **Mushrooms are closer to animals than to plants** — they don't make their own food.
- **Seaweed** does photosynthesis like a plant, and it still isn't a plant. Doing photosynthesis alone doesn't make something a plant!
- A lichen is "2 in 1": the **alga makes food** and the **fungus gives shelter and water**.

## 6. Plants 3 — Plant Structures and Groups

Think of it as a family tree 🌳 — each question splits the plants into two groups:

```
                         PLANTS
                  /                  \
        NON-VASCULAR               VASCULAR
      (mosses, liverworts)        /          \
                           SEEDLESS          WITH SEEDS
                          (ferns)          /           \
                                  GYMNOSPERMS      ANGIOSPERMS
                                (pines, cones)    (flowering plants)
                                                   /           \
                                               MONOCOTS       DICOTS
```

### Vascular vs. non-vascular
| Vascular | Non-vascular |
|---|---|
| Have **tubes** (vascular tissue) to carry water and food | **No tubes** |
| **Xylem** carries **water** up from the roots · **Phloem** carries **food** (sugar) to the rest of the plant | Water moves slowly from cell to cell |
| Can grow **tall** | Stay very **small and low** to the ground |
| Have real roots, stems, and leaves | No real roots, stems, or leaves |
| Trees, ferns, flowers, grass | **Mosses, liverworts** — live in **wet, shady** places |

🧠 Trick: **X**ylem = water goes up like an **X**-press elevator ⬆️ · **Ph**loem = **f**ood (the "ph" sounds like "f").

### Seedless vs. seed plants
| Seedless | Seed plants |
|---|---|
| Reproduce with **spores** | Reproduce with **seeds** |
| Need **water/moisture** to reproduce | Don't need water to reproduce |
| **Ferns** (vascular), mosses (non-vascular) | Pines, flowers, trees, grass |

⚠️ **Twist:** **ferns** are **vascular** but **seedless** (they use spores, the brown dots under the leaves).

### Gymnosperms vs. angiosperms
| Gymnosperms | Angiosperms |
|---|---|
| "**Naked** seeds" — seeds are **not covered** by a fruit | Seeds are **covered / protected inside a fruit** |
| Seeds grow in **cones** 🌲 | Have **flowers** 🌸 and **fruits** 🍎 |
| **No flowers** | The **largest** group of plants |
| Pine, fir, cypress, cedar (often evergreen) | Roses, apple trees, corn, grass, oak trees |

🧠 Trick: **Gym**nosperm → at the **gym**, seeds show up "naked" (without a coat) 😅 · **Angio**sperm → the seed is in a "**vessel**" (a fruit).

### Monocots vs. dicots (angiosperms only) — look at the veins!
| | **Monocot** | **Dicot** |
|---|---|---|
| **Leaf veins** | **Parallel** (straight lines, side by side) ‖‖‖ | **Branched / net-like** (like a web) 🕸️ |
| Seed leaves (cotyledons) | **1** (mono = one) | **2** (di = two) |
| Flower parts | In multiples of **3** | In multiples of **4 or 5** |
| Roots | **Fibrous** (many thin roots) | **Taproot** (one big main root) |
| Examples | Grass, corn, rice, wheat, lilies, palm trees, bananas | Roses, beans, sunflowers, oak trees, tomatoes, mint |

⚠️ **Twists:**
- The exam asks for **vein pattern** → **parallel = monocot**, **net = dicot**.
- 🧠 Trick: a **mono**cot leaf looks like **lines on a road** (all straight); a **di**cot leaf looks like a **tree with branches**.
- Grass and **corn** are **monocots** (and they are flowering plants — their flowers are just small!).

## 7. Plants 4 — Roots, Stems, Leaves, and Photosynthesis

### The 3 main parts
| Part | Jobs | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Roots** | 1. **Anchor** the plant in the ground · 2. **Absorb water and minerals** · 3. Some **store food** | Carrots and radishes are roots that store food |
| **Stems** | 1. **Support** the plant (hold it up) · 2. **Transport** water and food (xylem and phloem) · 3. Some store food | A tree trunk is a stem; a potato is actually a stem! |
| **Leaves** | Make food by **photosynthesis** · Let gases in and out through **stomata** (tiny holes) | Lettuce and spinach are leaves |

### Label a leaf
| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Tip** (apex) | The pointy **end** of the leaf |
| **Midrib** | The **main, central vein** that runs down the middle of the leaf |
| **Veins** | The smaller "tubes" that branch out and carry water and food |
| **Cuticle** | A **waxy, waterproof layer** on top of the leaf that **stops it from losing water** (it's why leaves look shiny) |
| Blade | The flat, wide part of the leaf |
| Petiole | The little stalk that connects the leaf to the stem |
| Margin | The edge of the leaf |

🧠 Trick: **Mid**rib = in the **mid**dle, like your **spine**. **Cuticle** = like a **coat of wax** (or a raincoat).

### Chlorophyll
- A **green pigment** inside **chloroplasts** (in leaf cells).
- It **absorbs sunlight** (the energy to make food).
- It's why plants are **green** (it reflects green light).

### Photosynthesis (basic)
Plants **make their own food (glucose/sugar)** using light energy.

> **Carbon dioxide + Water + Light energy → Glucose (sugar) + Oxygen**
> (the chlorophyll captures the light)

| Ingredient | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| **Carbon dioxide (CO₂)** | The air, through the **stomata** |
| **Water (H₂O)** | The soil, through the **roots** (then up the xylem) |
| **Light** | The **sun**, captured by **chlorophyll** |

| Product | What happens to it |
|---|---|
| **Glucose** | The plant's **food** (energy for growing) |
| **Oxygen (O₂)** | Released into the air — the oxygen **we breathe**! |

⚠️ **Twists:**
- Plants take in **carbon dioxide** and give out **oxygen** — the opposite of what we breathe.
- Plants **don't get their food from the soil**. From the soil they get **water and minerals**; the **food** they make themselves.
- Photosynthesis happens mostly in the **leaves**, because that's where there's the most chlorophyll.
- A potato is a **stem**, a carrot is a **root**.

## 8. Plants 5 — Reproduction and Life Cycles

Plants continue life in **two ways**:

| Sexual reproduction | Asexual reproduction |
|---|---|
| Needs **two parents** (pollen + ovule) | Needs only **one parent** |
| Babies are **different** from the parents (a mix) | Babies are **identical copies** (clones) |
| Flowers → seeds | Parts of the plant grow into a new plant |

### Flowers (the reproductive part of angiosperms)
| Part | Male or female | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Stamen** = anther + filament | ♂ **Male** | The **anther** makes **pollen**; the filament holds it up |
| **Pistil** = stigma + style + ovary | ♀ **Female** | The **stigma** (sticky top) catches pollen; the **style** is the tube; the **ovary** holds the **ovules** (future seeds) |
| **Petals** | — | Colorful, to **attract pollinators** |
| **Sepals** | — | Green leaves that **protect the bud** |

🧠 Trick: **Stamen** → "**Stay-men**" = the male part. **Pistil** → the female part in the middle.

### Life cycle of a flowering plant 🔄
1. **Seed** — contains a baby plant (embryo) + stored food.
2. **Germination** — the seed "wakes up" with **water, oxygen, and the right temperature** and starts to sprout.
3. **Seedling** — a young plant with its first leaves.
4. **Adult plant** — grows and makes **flowers**.
5. **Pollination** — **pollen** moves from the anther to the **stigma** (by **bees, butterflies, birds, bats, wind, or water**).
6. **Fertilization** — the pollen joins the **ovule** inside the ovary.
7. **Seed and fruit** — the ovule becomes a **seed**, and the ovary becomes the **fruit**.
8. **Seed dispersal** — seeds are spread far away (by **wind** — dandelion; **water** — coconut; **animals** — eating fruit or sticking to fur) → and the cycle starts again!

### Other ways to continue life
- **Spores:** **ferns and mosses** reproduce with spores instead of seeds (they need moist places).
- **Asexual / vegetative reproduction:**
  - **Runners** — strawberries 🍓 (stems that grow along the ground and make new plants)
  - **Tubers** — potatoes 🥔 (the "eyes" sprout into new plants)
  - **Bulbs** — onions, garlic, tulips 🧅
  - **Cuttings** — cut a piece of a plant, put it in water, and it grows roots

⚠️ **Twists:**
- **Pollination ≠ fertilization.** Pollination is pollen **arriving** at the stigma; fertilization is pollen **joining** the ovule. Pollination comes **first**.
- **Seeds don't need light to germinate** — they need **water, oxygen, and warmth** (they have stored food).
- The **fruit** comes from the **ovary**; the **seed** comes from the **ovule**.
- A **tomato** and a **cucumber** are fruits scientifically (they have seeds inside)!
- **Gymnosperms** don't have flowers — their seeds form in **cones**, and the wind carries their pollen.
